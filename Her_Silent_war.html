Two Seconds of Eternity

The Ice and The Ember
While you were entirely lost in your two seconds of eternity, analyzing the tragic mathematics of the distance between us... I was drowning in the exact same frozen heartbeat, silently screaming for you to finally be brave.

I. THE CHAOS OF CLASS 12 AND THE GHOST

Kathmandu was never meant to be a romantic escape for me; it was an iron forge. I was there for my Class 12 studies, suffocating under the immense weight of board exams, endless early-morning tuitions, and the crushing expectations of my family. My days were a brutal, blinding blur of heavy textbooks, the relentless blare of Ring Road traffic, and the bitter, exhausted taste of cheap coffee. I was supposed to be forging my future, leaving the quiet dust of our hometown—and everything it contained—far behind.

But memory is a cruel, relentless thing. No matter how many complex formulas I memorized, or how many pages of notes I furiously scribbled, my mind kept drifting back to a quiet boy in Room 204. I used to catch you looking at me when you thought my eyes were turned away. I knew you were brilliant. I knew you lived entirely inside your own head, locked away in an analytical fortress of your own design. You were a creature of pure logic, and I always wondered what catastrophic event it would take for you to finally break your own rules and speak to me.

II. The Collision of Worlds

Then came that suffocating, apocalyptic afternoon. The air in Kathmandu was thick with golden dust and the unbearable heat of a thousand running engines. I was sitting by the window of a packed, miserable local bus, staring completely blankly at the gridlock, utterly exhausted by the sheer volume of the city.

Suddenly, the relentless momentum of Kathmandu was forced to a dead halt. Another commuter bus crept up and ground to a stop mere inches from mine. Seeking a momentary escape from the stifling heat of the cabin, I turned my head to look blindly out the grimy glass. I expected to see nothing but another tired, nameless stranger in a city of three million. But the universe has a violently tragic sense of humor.

There you were.

III. The Ice and The Ember

The roaring chaos of the street instantly vanished. The heavy, suspended dust seemed to freeze mid-air. For two agonizing, infinite seconds, our eyes locked across the narrow two-foot abyss of empty air.

While you were lost in your two seconds of eternity, I was completely paralyzed in mine. In that single, suspended breath, the noisy streets evaporated. I remembered absolutely everything. I remembered the chalk dust suspended in the light of our old classroom. I remembered the heavy, unspoken tension that always hummed like a live wire between us.

When our eyes locked through that dirty glass, my heart stopped beating. I had spent years convincing myself that your icy, introverted nature meant I was nothing more than a ghost to you. But in those two microscopic seconds, your impenetrable fortress completely shattered. I saw the pure, violent shock on your face. I saw the raw, unfiltered desperation in your eyes, mirroring the exact same terrifying fire burning inside my own chest. You weren't looking at me; you were looking into my soul. In that suspended heartbeat, I finally realized the truth: you hadn't been ignoring me. You were just as terrified of the fire as I was.

IV. The Anchor of Cowardice

For a beautiful, fleeting moment, I thought you were finally going to move. I thought you would smash the glass, shout my name, or do something to break the terrible spell that bound us. My hand instinctively pressed flat against my window. I wanted to bridge the gap. I wanted to drag you out of your silence.

But then, the traffic light turned green.

The city violently reclaimed us. The bus lurched forward with a harsh, metallic scream. I gasped, frozen in my seat, entirely unable to overcome the old, familiar paralysis that anchored me down. I watched your face blur as my bus accelerated, tearing me away from the only real thing in the entire sprawling valley. I twisted violently in my seat, watching your silhouette fade into the exhaust smoke until you were entirely swallowed by the frantic river of metal.

V. The Midnight Standoff

That night in my tiny hostel room, the silence was deafening. My Class 12 books lay completely abandoned on the floor. The only light in the pitch-black room was the harsh, blue glow of my phone screen. I had your profile open. I saw the green dot. You were online.

My thumbs hovered over the keyboard, trembling. I typed, “I saw you today.” And then, swallowed by doubt, I deleted it. I typed, “Why didn't you say anything?” I deleted that, too. I was consumed by a fiery anger—at myself for being a coward, but mostly at you. You were the logical one. You were the one who could analyze systems and figure anything out. Why couldn't you figure us out?

I threw my phone onto the bed, staring fiercely at the ceiling. No, I told myself, letting my stubborn pride take over. I shouldn't have to chase him. If this meant anything to him, if that look in his eyes was real, he will find the courage to message me first.

I waited for hours in the dark. I watched your active status pulse, then fade into a timestamp. The message never came. You never fought for it. And so, neither did I.

VI. A Stubborn Refusal to Forget

I used to foolishly believe that if two people were meant to be, the universe would somehow magically align the stars and push them together. I thought the world was a beautiful, poetic place that rewarded silent, patient longing.

I was so entirely wrong. The universe isn't a fairy tale; it’s a chaotic, indifferent machine. It will put the person you want exactly two feet away from you, just to brutally test you both. And if you do not reach out, if you do not violently fight for your own happiness with your own two hands, the universe will ruthlessly pull them away forever.

I accept that tragic reality now. I accept that our silence was a choice we both actively made in those two seconds. But even as I close my textbooks and force myself to walk into my future, there is one thing I have stubbornly refused to accept.

I have not accepted forgetting you. Until you find the courage to hit 'Enter', I will keep your memory perfectly alive in the space between the ice and the fire.

The city roared, the engines screamed,

I found the boy of whom I dreamed.

Through dusty glass, our gazes crossed,

Two seconds passed, and both were lost.

Your fortress made of quiet ice,

Demanded such a heavy price.

But through the pane, the walls came down,

And showed the fire where you'd drown.

I held my breath, I prayed you'd speak,

But both our cowardly hearts were weak.

The screen glowed blue beside my bed,

With all the screaming words unsaid.

I waited for your light to show,

I needed you to let me know.

But silence won the bitter fight,

And swallowed us into the night.

I've learned the stars won't fight for me,

If I refuse to cross the sea.

But though our paths may never set,

I stubbornly refuse to forget.
