Two Corners, Two Diverged Paths

Setting: Room 204, final period • Lesson: Frost’s The Road Not Taken

You sat in the far corner near the window, wrapped in the soft, golden light of the late afternoon. I sat in the opposite corner, quiet and still in the shadows of the back row. Between us was a room full of desks, but the real distance felt endless.

At the front of the classroom, the teacher’s voice echoed softly, reading through Robert Frost’s The Road Not Taken. But I wasn't listening to the lesson. I was entirely lost in you.

While the teacher spoke of choices and diverging paths, I was quietly building an entire lifetime with you in my head—imagining shared mornings, quiet conversations, and a world where I had the courage to actually matter to you. To me, you were the center of everything. To you, I was just a background character—a nameless face in the crowd, completely invisible.

I spent the entire hour staring across that room, asking myself the same desperate question: What if I just walked over? What if I told you how I feel?

When the bell finally rang, you gathered your things, laughed at something a classmate said, and stepped out into the hallway without looking back. You took your path, completely untouched by the moment. I stayed behind, carrying the silent weight of a confession that only ever lived in my imagination.

Loving you from afar was the road I never had the courage to walk. And that unchosen path—the silent one across the classroom—is the one that still lingers.
